## New in 1.52.0: the structure, generated from your walls

- **Generate structure** (Draw › Generate structure…, or the new **Structure** section of the side panel) works out the whole frame from your walls, doors, windows, rooms and stairs on every floor, in one step you can undo.
- **Columns** go at every corner, tee and cross of the walls and along long walls (15' apart at most), never in a door or window, with one each side of wide openings (8' and up). They stack floor to floor: a column an upper floor needs runs down every floor to its footing. Sizes follow the floors they carry (9" × 12" up to two storeys, 12" × 12" for three, 12" × 18" for four or more, larger where the load needs it).
- **Beams** on every wall line between columns, at the top of the walls (span ÷ 12 deep), and under upper-floor walls with nothing below them; **secondary beams** across rooms wider than 16'; **beams round stair openings**; **plinth beams** under the ground floor's walls and **tie beams** joining the footings.
- **Lintels** over every door, window and opening, sitting 9" on the wall past each side, 6" or 9" deep by span, joined where two nearly meet. They follow the openings as you move them, and each door or window can turn its lintel off or change its depth.
- **Slabs** over each floor's rooms out to the walls' outer faces, with the stair and double-height openings cut.
- **Footings:** a pad under each column, sized for the load and your soil (1.5 tons/sq ft unless you change it), joined into one where two would overlap and kept inside the plot. Boundary walls get strip footings.
- **Load-bearing brick** instead of a frame: no columns, strip footings under the walls, a plinth band, a roof band and (seismic zone 2B and up) a lintel band.
- **Marks** (C1, B2, PB1, TB1, F1, L1...) by size, a **structure schedule** (File › Export structure schedule) with each mark's size, number, length and concrete, and **Foundation plan** and **Framing plans** sheets in the drawings.
- **Regenerate** after changing the plan: anything you moved, resized or locked is kept, and the rest is worked out again round it. A check says when the structure is out of date.
- **Only the structure in 3D:** walls, furniture and the ground go, so you see the frame down to the footings. On the plan it shows the footings and the marks.
- **Checks** for beams spanning too far, slabs too thin for their room, floating columns, and columns in doorways, rooms or stairs.
- The structure goes into **DXF** (its own layers), **IFC** (footings, lintels and beams by kind) and **quantities and cost** (footings, lintels and columns down to their footings, with the brickwork they replace taken out).
- Sizes are rules of thumb for drawing and costing: **a structural engineer must check them.**
